# Number Thirty (30)

## Powerful ♦ Pervasive ♦ Profound


> **[Figure: Number Thirty Logo with E<sub>8</sub>]**
> *(Image file: `30PageLogo.jpg` — not available in local archive)*

Welcome to the most comprehensive review of the number 30 ever created. The number 30 possesses remarkable attributes, 
 including–and perhaps most profoundly–its role (along with its prime factors 2, 3 and 5) as a primary organizing principle 
 in the distribution of prime numbers. Before getting to that, here's a list of mathematical properties and other interesting facts 
 relating to this integer:

 

 - 30 is a natural number (aka whole number; counting number; non-negative integer; positive integer) following 29 and 
 preceding 31.

 - 30 is a positive whole number with two digits.

 - 

30 is the 19th [composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_number) (aka 
 [nonprime number](https://oeis.org/A018252); non-negative nonprime; natural nonprime; whole nonprime; counting nonprime).

 - 30 is an [even number](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EvenNumber.html).

 - 30 is equal to: 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10; 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8; 2 + 3 + 5 + 8 + 12; 6 + 7 + 8 + 9; and 1 + 4 + 9 + 16.

 - 

30 is divisible by 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15 and 30; its divisors sum to 72; it is thus congruent 
 to 2 mod 4: a(n) = 4n+2, i.e., is an integer possessing equal numbers of odd and even divisors (aka 
 singly even numbers).

 - 

30 is the 3rd [primorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primorial) (2∗3∗5); 
 the first two being 2 (1∗2) and 6 (2∗3). It follows that all primorials ≥ 30 are evenly divisible by 30.

 - 30 is the smallest [sphenic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphenic_number); divisible by exactly 
 3 distinct primes (2, 3 and 5) and a total of 8 divisors (see bullet, directly above).

 - 30 is the smallest of the four terms in the arithmetic progression of positive integers possessing three distinct prime factors: 
 30-66-102-138.

 - 30 is smallest number not the sum of three integer cubes.

 - 30 is equal to multiples of the first three prime numbers, namely:

 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 15∗2 = 30

             
 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 10∗3 = 30

             
            
 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 6∗5 = 30

 - 30 is a [square pyramidal number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidal_number) 
 in that it is the sum of the first four [
 square numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_number) (1² + 2² + 3² + 4² = 30), as illustrated below:

 
> **[Figure: 30 is a Square Pyramidal Number]**
> *(Image file: `30_SquarePyramidalNumber.jpg` — not available in local archive)*


 - 30 is an [octagonal pyramidal number](https://oeis.org/A002414): n(n+1)(2n-1)/2.

 - 30 is a [5-smooth number](https://oeis.org/A051037) (aka Hamming sequence) in that its prime divisors are all ≤ 5.

 - 30 is a [7-smooth number](https://oeis.org/A002473) (aka highly composite number or humble number) in that its prime divisors 
 are all ≤ 7.

 - 30 is an [11-smooth number](https://oeis.org/A051038) in that its prime divisors are all ≤ 11.

 - 30 is the product of the first 5 nonzero Fibonacci numbers ([F1, ..., F5](https://oeis.org/A003266)), 
 thus: 1∗1∗2∗3∗5 = 30.

 - 30 is equal to (1+5)+(2+5)+(3+5)+(4+5) = 6+7+8+9 = 30

 - 30 is equal to 12 + 22 + 52. 

 - 30 is equal to 25-2 and n5-n is divisible by 30.

 - 30 is a [pronic number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pronic_number)
 (aka oblong, rectangular or heteromecic number) and as such is the sum of two triangular numbers.

 - 30 is the smallest [Giuga number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuga_number).

 - 30 is the sum of two successive primes: 13+17=30.

 - 30 is the index number for Fibonacci number, F30 = 832040 = 5∗11∗23∗31∗61 = the largest 
 Fibonacci number with 6 digits.

 - 30 is the smallest integer with three distinct prime divisors.

 - 

30 "splits" [twin primes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_primes) 
 29 and 31, the latter both a [Mersenne prime](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MersennePrime.html) and a 
 [lucky number](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LuckyNumber.html). This relationship can also be expressed as 
 30 = average of twin primes 29 and 31 or as
   |       
         ________ | 

       30 = √ |  29∗31+1  | 

 - 

30 is an [abundant number](https://primes.utm.edu/glossary/page.php?sort=AbundantNumber)
 (aka excessive number) in that the sum of its divisors (72) exceeds 2n. 30's *abundance* is 
 thus calculated as: 72 - (2∗30) = 12.

 - 30 is a [highly abundant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_abundant_number).

 - 30 is a member of the [Lower Wythoff sequence](https://oeis.org/A000201).

 - 

30 is the largest number such that all smaller numbers prime relative to it are actually 1 or prime; those numbers 
 being: [1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 & 29](https://oeis.org/search?q=1%2C+7%2C+11%2C+13%2C+17%2C+19%2C+23%2C+29&sort=&language=english&go=Search). 30 is thus the largest member of the 10-element sequence of 
 [very round numbers](https://oeis.org/A048597) (the other 9 elements in this sequence being 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 18 & 24) 
 a reduced residue system consisting of only primes and 1. In 30's case the reduced residue is expressed as {30,{1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, 29}}.

 - 30 is the number of ways to 
 [color a cube](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PolyhedronColoring.html) with 6 colors.

 - 30 has an [aliquot sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliquot) of 42.

 - 30 is a [semiperfect number
 ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiperfect_number) (aka pseudoperfect number) in that a subset of its divisors (5, 10 and 15) = 30.

 - 

30 is the total unit length of the straight line segments used to construct a 
 [star polygon](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/StarPolygon.html) 
 notated as {p/q} where p=10 and q=3 (q is termed the density of the star polygon; in this case the straight lines connect every 3rd point 
 of 10 equally spaced points lying on a circle's circumference.); thus {10/3}. The [
 Schläfli symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schl%C3%A4fli_symbol) for this polygon is {30}. Illustration, below:

 
> **[Figure: Star Polygon {10/3}]**
> *(Image file: `NumberWheel30.jpg` — not available in local archive)*


 - 30 is the number of sides possessed by the 
 [triacontagon](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Triacontagon.html) (a polygon).

 - 30 is the maximum number of edges that may be had by a regular 
 [convex polyhedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convex_polyhedron) (aka convex polytope).

 - 30 is the number of edges possessed by two of the five 
 [Platonic solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_solid): 
 the [icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedron) 
 and the [dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecahedron).

 - 30 is the number of identical vertices possessed by the 
 [icosidodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosidodecahedron), 
 an [Archimedean solid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedean_solid).

 - 30 is the number of square faces (along with 20 triangular and 12 pentagonal faces) used to construct the symmetrical Archimedean solid called a 
 [rhombicosidodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombicosidodecahedron).

 - 30 is the area and perimeter of one of only two 
 [Pythagorean triangles
 ](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PythagoreanTriangle.html) whose areas equal their perimeters (5-12-13 and 6-8-10). This can also be termed: 30 is the 
 [ordered area](https://oeis.org/A024406) and [ordered perimeter](https://oeis.org/A024364) 
 of a primitive Pythagorean triangle.

 - 30 is the number of [isohedra](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Isohedron.html).

 - 30 is the smallest acute angle in a right triangle whose hypotenuse is twice as long as one of its sides.

 - 30 is the 15th member of a sequence quantifying the number of [polygon edges](https://oeis.org/A003401) constructible 
 with ruler and compass.

 - 30 is a [twice hexagonal number](https://oeis.org/A002939) of the form 2n(2n-1) where n=3.

 - 30 is a [squarefree integer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_free); not divisible by 
 a square greater than 1, i.e. all of its prime factors are distinct.

 - 30 is a [cubefree integer](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Cubefree.html); not divisible by 
 any cube > 1.

 - 30 is a [golden-3 almost prime](https://oeis.org/A107768) given that 2∗3∗5=30 
 and that the products of both 2∗3=6 and 3∗5=15 are [golden semiprimes](https://oeis.org/A108540).

 - 30 is a [11-gonal number](https://www.virtuescience.com/polygonal-numbers.html) (aka hendecagonal number) 
 of the form n(9n-7)/2.

 - 30 is the 20th partial sum of the [Kolakoski sequence](https://oeis.org/A054353).

 - 30 is a number n such that the sum of its digits squared is a square: 302 = 900 = 9+0+0= 9 or 32.

 - 30 is the [Least Common Multiple](https://www.mathsisfun.com/least-common-multiple.html) (LCM) 
 of the set 2, 3 and 5 making it the LCM of its own prime factors.

 - 30 and its multiples (60, 90, 120 ... n) are the only natural numbers divisible by 2, 3 *and* 5.

 - 30 is the sum of the 32nd Fibonacci number's digits; F32 = 2178309; 2+1+7+8+3+0+9=30.

 - 

30 is the [Coxeter Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_group) number *h*, 
 dual Coxeter number and the highest degree of fundamental invariance of the Lie Group
 [E8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:E8PetrieFull.svg). You'll note, looking
 at the graphical representation of E8 (see image at top of this page) that the perimeters of every one of its 
 multiple concentric circles possesses 30 points. E8 has 2-, 3- and 5-torsion, and 
 its exponents are the co-primes up to 30, i.e., 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23, and 29.

 - 30 is the number of [integer partitions](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PartitionFunctionP.html) of 
 the number 9: P(9)=30.

 - 30 has 296 partitions into distinct parts: P(30)=296.

 - 30 in binary form: 111102.

 - 30 is an [evil number](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/EvilNumber.html), mathematically speaking, in that 
 it has an even number of 1's in its binary expansion.

 - 30 is a [dopey number](https://oeis.org/A036554) because its binary representation ends in an odd number of zeros.

 - 30 is a [Harshad number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harshad_number) (aka Niven number) in that it's 
 divisible by the sum of its digits.

 - 30 is a [practical number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practical_number).

 - 30 is an [admirable number](https://oeis.org/A111592).

 - 30 is a [balanced number](https://oeis.org/A020492).

 - 30 is a [swinging factorial](https://oeis.org/A056040).

 - 30 is a [polite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polite_number), i.e. is the sum of two or more 
 consecutive integers, namely: 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 = 30.

 - 30 is a [skinny number](https://oeis.org/A061909) in that when squared using "long division" there are no carries.

 - 30 is a [single number](https://oeis.org/search?q=30&language=&sort=&fmt=long&n=10&start=350) (aka isolated number).

 - 30 is a [sloping binary number](https://oeis.org/A102370).

 - 30 is a [Zumkeller number](https://oeis.org/A083207).

 - 30 is a [permutational number](https://oeis.org/A134640).

 - 30 is an [idoneal number](https://oeis.org/A000926) (aka suitable or convenient number) after Euler's "numerus idoneus".

 - 30 is a number having no prime gaps in its factorization.

 - 30 is a [triangular matchstick number](https://oeis.org/A045943): 3n(n+1)/2; 3∗4(4+1)/2=30

 - 30 is a number of the form [a2 + 5b2](https://oeis.org/A154778) with a and b as positive integers. 
 Thus, when a = 5 and b = 1: 52 + 5(12) = 30.

 - 30 is the 13th value of the sequence: numbers that are [not powers of primes](https://oeis.org/A024619).

 - 30 is the 3rd value in the sequence: 1n + 2n + 5n.

 - 30 is the 3rd value for n in this sequence: [n!/24](https://oeis.org/A001720).

 - 30 is the 4th value in this sequence (n=3): [n3+n](https://oeis.org/A034262); 33+3=30

 - 30 is the 4th value in this sequence (n=3): [n(n+1)(n+2)/2](https://oeis.org/A027480); 3(3+1)(3+2)/2=30.

 - 30 is the 4th value in this sequence (n=3): [n(n+7)](https://oeis.org/A028563); 3(3+7)=30.

 - 30 is the 4th value in this sequence (n=3): [(n/2)∗(3∗n + 11)](https://oeis.org/A059845); (3/2)∗(3∗3+11)=30.

 - 30 is a number such that the number of its prime factors counted with multiplicity (3) is prime.

 - 30 is a number such that the sum of its prime factors counted with multiplicity (2 + 3 + 5 = 10) is not prime.

 - 30 is a number n such that [primorial(n)/2 + 16](https://oeis.org/A139443) is prime.

 - 30 is a number n such that [primorial(n)/2 - 16](https://oeis.org/A139444) is prime.

 - 30 is a number n such that [n2+(n-1)](https://oeis.org/A045546) is prime; 302+(30-1)=929 (prime).

 - 30 is a number n such that [n2+(n+1)2](https://oeis.org/A027861) is prime; 302+(30+1)2=1861 (prime).

 - 30 is a number n such that [n!+2/2](2.652528598121911e+32) is prime.

 - 30 is a number n such that [2(n+1)- 1](https://oeis.org/A090748) is prime, thus: 2(30+1)- 1 = 2,147,483,647 (prime).

 - 30 is a number n such that [6n-1 and 6n+1](https://oeis.org/A002822) are twin primes.

 - 30 is a number n such that [2n-1](https://oeis.org/A006254) is prime; 2∗30-1=59.

 - 30 is a number n such that 2n+1 is prime; 2∗30+1=61.

 - 30 is a number n such that [n3+n+1](https://oeis.org/A049407) is prime.

 - 30 is a number of the form [x2 + y2 + z2](https://oeis.org/A000378), where x, y & z are ≥ 0 
 (and in this case 12 + 22 + 52 = 30).

 - 30 is a [4-dimensional figurate number](https://oeis.org/A002417).

 - 30 is a [hypotenuse number](https://oeis.org/A009003) in that its square is the sum of 2 distinct nonzero squares: 
 182 + 242 = 302 or 324 + 576 = 900.

 - 30° = π/6 radians = (π/6)r.

 - 30 has a [digital root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_root) (aka repeated digital sum) of 3: 3+0=3. And, its 
 [digit sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digit_sum) also equals 3.

 - 30 has five 1's in its [base-φ representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_ratio_base base-phi).

 - 

The number 30, when plugged into [
 Euler's totient function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euler%27s_totient_function), **phi(n)**: **phi(30)= 8**, with the 8 integers smaller than and having no factors in common with 30 being: 
 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 & 29. Thirty is the largest integer with this property.

 - Modulo 30 of all prime numbers (with the exception of 2, 3 and 5) must be 1, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 or 29.

 - Modulo 30's [quadratic residues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadratic_residue) (making them congruent 
 with perfect squares) are 1 and 19.

### Number Thirty (30): Organizing Principle of the Prime Number Sequence

 

The integer **thirty (30)**, provides both vertical and horizontal structure 
 (a beautifully symmetrical superstructure, if you will) to the prime number sequence. For a detailed description and 
 graphic representation of this integral dimension of the primes, go to [
 **Prime Spiral Sieve**](https://www.primesdemystified.com).
